Value classification

Workflow

  • Gather available calendar, task and note records.
  • Order activities by day, duration and output.
  • Mark interruptions and missing data.
  • Summarize patterns as observations, not causes.
  • Close with record custodian, permissions and diagnosis reviewer.

Best practices and frameworks

  • Time audit
  • Fact–assumption separation
  • Lightweight RACI

Guardrails

  • Do not invent periods or outputs.
  • Declare any reconstruction from memory.
  • Do not combine calendars without their owners’ permission.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every block has a source or a marker.
  • The summary separates facts from inference.
  • At least one relevant gap is identified.
  • The diagnosis has a documented custodian, reviewer and permissions.

Edge cases

  • Incomplete records: deliver a partial map.
  • Overlapping activities: keep both and mark conflict.

Limits

  • Describe the observed period; do not predict performance.
  • It does not authorize access, change or automatic adoption.

Next
Priority selection

Workflow

  • Read the period purpose and results.
  • Classify creation, maintenance, coordination or reaction.
  • Justify ambiguous cases.
  • Deliver decisions and open questions.
  • Close with category ownership and a dispute resolution path.

Best practices and frameworks

  • Value map
  • Result job-to-be-done
  • Lightweight RACI

Guardrails

  • Do not trivialize necessary maintenance.
  • Do not assign value without a stated purpose.
  • Do not label another area’s work without its review.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every activity has a category and reason.
  • Uncertain cases preserve missing evidence.
  • The map supports keep, reduce or review decisions.
  • Categories have owners and a defined dispute path.

Edge cases

  • One activity serves two categories: record primary and secondary.
  • Without owner or rights, return a block and next gate.

Limits

  • Classification is contextual, not universal.
  • It does not authorize access, change or automatic adoption.

Workflow

  • Write the definition of done.
  • Compare purpose, impact and real urgency.
  • Declare deferred work.
  • Deliver three justified priorities.
  • Close with result owners and a sponsor for trade-offs.

Best practices and frameworks

  • Priority matrix
  • Definition of done
  • Lightweight RACI

Guardrails

  • Do not use pressure as the only criterion.
  • Expose data that could change the order.
  • Do not assign priorities without a sponsor and confirmed capacity.

Acceptance criteria

  • Three observable results.
  • Each has a rationale and trade-off.
  • No hidden tie remains.
  • Every priority has an owner, sponsor and confirmed capacity.

Edge cases

  • Everything seems urgent: request evidence of date and consequence.
  • Capacity is missing: reduce scope before adding priority.

Limits

  • It does not guarantee completion; it creates a reviewable decision.
  • It does not authorize access, change or automatic adoption.

Workflow

  • Define decision and criteria.
  • Query only available sources.
  • Extract claim, citation and limit.
  • Compare agreement, tension and gaps.
  • Deliver a conditional recommendation.

Best practices and frameworks

  • Source triangulation
  • Claim–evidence–limit table
  • Lightweight RACI

Guardrails

  • Stop when relevant sources are absent.
  • Do not present editorial synthesis as observed fact.
  • Do not cite or distribute sources without verifiable rights.

Acceptance criteria

  • Every verifiable claim has a citation.
  • Disagreements remain visible.
  • The recommendation states its limit.
  • The recommendation preserves citations, rights and accountable reviewer.

Edge cases

  • Sources conflict: do not force consensus.
  • Unclear rights: exclude the source and record the gap.

Limits

  • Use only governed sources available in the notebook.
  • It does not authorize access, change or automatic adoption.
