Chapter 01

Choose the case

Principle: Start where failure is visible and recoverable.

A stable case tests the contract without mixing novelty, sensitive authority and hidden dependencies.

Decision it resolvesChoose or reject the case before designing autonomy.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. Compare three candidates.
  2. Reject external effects and high sensitivity.
  3. Assign sponsor and recovery owner.

Checklist

  • Repeatable, bounded case.
  • Sponsor and recovery assigned.
Decision rule
If you cannot observe and recover, it is not the first pilot.
Example
Synthetic example: sandbox classification, without replying.
Antipattern
Choosing the most complex process to signal ambition.
Expected evidence
Suitability matrix and rejection reasons.
Limit
Suitability applies only to the assessed case and scope.

Chapter 02

Define owner and result

Principle: Human accountability must be nameable.

The agent produces or proposes; a person answers for the objective and a separate review may test the result.

Decision it resolvesAssign decision, review and acceptance before the brief.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. Name the accountable owner.
  2. Name a reviewer when needed.
  3. Separate output owner and reviewer.

Checklist

  • Explicit owner.
  • Acceptance separate from production.
Decision rule
No agent is owner, approver or source of authority.
Example
Synthetic example: Operations owns the decision; Quality reviews it.
Antipattern
Using “the team” as owner without a name or gate.
Expected evidence
Accountability card and verdicts.
Limit
Human review cannot repair a vague contract by itself.

Chapter 03

Prepare context and sources

Principle: Use only sufficient, traceable context.

Authorized sources support the plan; material gaps are declared and block the affected step.

Decision it resolvesAuthorize, limit or exclude every source.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. Inventory sources and rights.
  2. State currency and limits.
  3. Record source custody and rights.

Checklist

  • Source tied to the case.
  • Rights and gaps recorded.
Decision rule
An inference never replaces a required source.
Example
Synthetic example: use an approved export and exclude attachments without rights.
Antipattern
Granting broad access “just in case.”
Expected evidence
Source package, limits and gaps.
Limit
More context may increase exposure without improving relevance.

Chapter 04

Limit tools and permissions

Principle: Authorize capabilities separately and by least privilege.

Reading, classifying, drafting and acting are distinct permissions with separate justification and revocation.

Decision it resolvesGrant only what a testable draft requires.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. Separate data, tools and actions.
  2. Mark prior approval.
  3. Name revocation authority.

Checklist

  • Permission tied to step.
  • Custody and revocation visible.
Decision rule
Anything undeclared remains prohibited.
Example
Synthetic example: read export and propose labels; never write to the system.
Antipattern
Confusing available access with granted authority.
Expected evidence
Permission matrix and approval log.
Limit
Least privilege limits impact; it does not remove review.

Chapter 05

Set restrictions and escalation

Principle: Every boundary needs an operational response.

Prohibitions, missing data and ambiguity must lead to stop, escalation or clarification, never improvised authority.

Decision it resolvesDefine which condition triggers each response.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. List prohibitions.
  2. Assign escalation.
  3. Define escalation for the gap.

Checklist

  • Observable stop.
  • Operating escalation defined.
Decision rule
When authority is insufficient, stop before acting.
Example
Synthetic example: escalate any request needing a reply; never send it.
Antipattern
Writing “be careful” without testable conduct.
Expected evidence
Condition-response table and exceptions.
Limit
Escalation must not become automatic approval.

Chapter 06

Approve the plan

Principle: Review the plan before any effect.

The gate corrects sequence, sources, permissions and reversibility while no environment has changed.

Decision it resolvesApprove, correct or reject with explicit criteria.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. Trace steps to sources.
  2. Trace steps to permissions.
  3. Assign plan-gate authority.

Checklist

  • Plan fits the brief.
  • Gate authority explicit.
Decision rule
An approved objective does not authorize every plan.
Example
Synthetic example: approve sandbox; reject production writes.
Antipattern
Reviewing only after the action occurred.
Expected evidence
Versioned plan and gate verdict.
Limit
An approved plan remains subject to exception and stop.

Chapter 07

Execute with evidence

Principle: A useful run can be reconstructed.

Input, version, decisions, actions, gates, exceptions and result form the minimum reviewable record.

Decision it resolvesAccept output only with sufficient traceability.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. Keep inputs and versions.
  2. Log decisions and gates.
  3. Give the log to the compliance reviewer.

Checklist

  • Identifiable input and version.
  • Log submitted for review.
Decision rule
Fluency is not evidence of compliance.
Example
Synthetic example: batch, labels, approvals and reviewer rejection.
Antipattern
Saving only the final output.
Expected evidence
Execution log and compliance table.
Limit
The log explains the case; it does not predict future performance.

Chapter 08

Handle exceptions

Principle: Design the exception before it occurs.

A safe response states how to stop, who reviews and how to discard output or restore state.

Decision it resolvesStop, escalate or recover according to the stated condition.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. Classify the exception.
  2. Activate owner and gate.
  3. Activate the recovery owner.

Checklist

  • Exception recorded.
  • Recovery owner active.
Decision rule
Never invent permission to resolve an exception.
Example
Synthetic example: freeze the batch and restore the prior queue.
Antipattern
Continuing to “finish” after traceability is lost.
Expected evidence
Exception, decision and recovery log.
Limit
Recovery does not remove the need to analyze cause.

Chapter 09

Decide continuity

Principle: Continuity is a decision, not momentum.

Keep, adjust or retire depends on contract compliance, exceptions and changes required by another context.

Decision it resolvesIssue a verdict and retain its evidence.
Go deeperHow to apply it

How to apply it

  1. Review done and limits.
  2. Assign corrections.
  3. Submit continuity to the sponsor.

Checklist

  • Verdict with reason.
  • Adoption has a new gate.
Decision rule
Do not widen scope or permission to rescue a failed pilot.
Example
Synthetic example: stop until rights are clear and a dependency has an owner.
Antipattern
Turning one good case into general authorization.
Expected evidence
Continuity decision and versioned package.
Limit
A pass confirms that contract in that case, not every use.

Glossary

Glossary

Mission
A bounded objective with context, restrictions and done.
Gate
A point where a person approves, corrects or stops.
Least privilege
Only the access or action required for the case.
Exception
A condition outside the expected flow that requires stop or escalation.
Recovery
A documented return to a safe state or disposal of the output.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

May an agent approve its own plan?

No. The plan is a proposal; approval belongs to the accountable person named in the contract.

What if a source is missing?

Declare the gap and stop the affected step. Never replace missing evidence with an assumption.

When should permissions expand?

Only for a concrete need, after risk review and new approval. A good pilot does not grant general authority.

What does a passed pilot mean?

It met done and its limits in that case; it does not validate the system for every context.

Checklist
  • Case, boundary, human owner and reviewer are written.
  • Sources, permissions and prohibitions are clear.
  • The plan was approved before the run.
  • Exceptions and recovery were recorded.
  • Continuity has a verdict, evidence and new authorization; this pilot grants no external effects.
