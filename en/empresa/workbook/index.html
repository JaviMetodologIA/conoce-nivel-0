Why it works

Acceptance criteria

  • Every idea in the summary carries a declared author or the explicit unattributed mark
  • Each pending decision lists a proposed owner and the operational risk of leaving it open
  • Someone who was not in the session can read the close and mark each point right or corrected

Edge cases

  • A single voice in the dictation: attribution is declared trivial and no participants are invented
  • Contradiction between two voices: it is kept and flagged, never averaged

Trade-offs

  • Attributing voices lengthens the summary in exchange for decisions that have an owner
  • This turn gives up the cleaning-level control the individual cell exposes: with several voices, aggressive compression erases authorship, so the level stays pinned to conservative. The original definition of done promised the person recognizes their intent and "discovers possibilities": here it is replaced by facts of the turn — ideas attributed or marked, five points confirmed, decisions with owner and risk — because discovery is neither produced nor verified by the prompt

Assumptions

  • The dictation comes from a real session and no record beyond the pasted text is available

Limits

  • It leaves the material confirmed and reviewable; it neither classifies the intent nor writes the study plan
  • It approves no decision and replaces nobody who must take one: it only names the proposed owner
