Letter from the founders

Four perspectives. One method.

We created MetodologIA to close the distance between wanting to move forward and knowing how. Learning is not collecting answers; it is asking better questions, working from sources and producing reviewable evidence.

Empowerment, efficiency, ecosystem and enablement meet here. This playbook does not promise automatic mastery; it offers a structure to understand, practise, verify and decide what to keep or change.

  • Javier MontañoChief Empowerment Officer
  • Daniel ZuluagaChief Efficiency Officer
  • Germán SepúlvedaChief Ecosystem Officer
  • Katherine OquendoChief Enablement Officer

10 progress scales

Locate current evidence before choosing the next practice.

  • 0–2: recognise, question and map.
  • 3–5: explain, apply and compare.
  • 6–9: defend, teach, adapt and renew.

Chapter 04

Learning modes

One structure can support learning, updating, changing fields or working together.

  • Self-learning with personal evidence.
  • Upskilling and reskilling tied to a real outcome.
  • Collaborative learning with shared sources and criteria.
05
Chapter 05

The fluency illusion

A fluent answer is not necessarily correct or sufficient.

  • Request citations and open the source.
  • Separate facts, inferences and gaps.
  • Test through retrieval, application and contrast.
06
Chapter 06

Three frameworks

Organise what exists, what you can do and how consistently you can do it.

  • Body of Knowledge: concepts, relationships and sources.
  • Capability model: actions you can perform.
  • Maturity model: repeatability, evidence and limits.
07
Chapter 07

Six study techniques

Useful practices when connected to purpose and evidence.

  • Retrieval and spaced practice.
  • Interleaving and elaboration.
  • Dual coding and concrete examples.
08
Chapter 08

Apprehend

Retain enough to retrieve, apply and defend without relying on the prompt.

  • Explain without notes and find where understanding breaks.
  • Produce a project, pitch, presentation or test.
  • Use feedback to correct the mental model.
09
Chapter 09

Deep research with AI

Research begins with the assignment, not the tool.

  • Declare purpose, scope, tensions and criteria.
  • Prioritise primary sources and real diversity.
  • Synthesize with citations, residual risk and the next decision.
10
Chapter 10

Integrity protocol

Trust comes from tracing what supports every claim.

  • Do not invent data or citations.
  • Do not treat model agreement as independent evidence.
  • Declare unknowns and stop when authority is missing.
11
Chapter 11

(R)Evolve

Reviewing what you learned also means releasing what no longer serves.

  • Keep what remains useful.
  • Update what is valuable but incomplete.
  • Replace or retire obsolete knowledge with a documented reason.
12
Chapter 12

Method tools

Choose the smallest combination that supports research, practice and verification.

  • A conversational AI to structure the assignment.
  • NotebookLM for working with a source base.
  • The open skill to repeat the workflow with guardrails.

NotebookLM

A space for conversing with selected sources and reviewing citations.

  • Centralise only sources you are allowed to use.
  • Configure a teacher, advisor or coach for the purpose.
  • Check responses against sources; the interface does not guarantee accuracy.

Learning workflows

Three indicative paths; time varies by topic and iteration.

  • Essential practice: about 30 minutes.
  • Ten-step route: 1 to 2 hours.
  • Extended route: 2 to 4 hours.
16
Chapter 16

Suggested routines

Cadence sustains the method better than a single session.

  • Daily curiosity: record one useful question.
  • Weekly apprehension: retrieve and explain without notes.
  • Periodic audit: review currency, usefulness and gaps.
17
Chapter 17

Learning standards

Practice ends when reviewable evidence exists, not when content was consumed.

  • Method first; tool second.
  • Sources before certainty.
  • Explain, apply and transfer before declaring progress.
18
Chapter 18

Essential glossary

Ten concepts for a shared language.

  • Blueprint · a verifiable research assignment.
  • BoK · a map of knowledge and sources.
  • Apprehend · retrieve and apply without dependency.
  • Triangulation · contrast independent evidence.
  • Sufficiency · evidence adequate to purpose and risk.
  • Teach-back · explain to reveal understanding.
  • Skill · reusable instructions and resources for a capability.
  • Prompt · an executable assignment for AI.
  • Primary source · evidence close to the fact or authority.
  • coverage_gap · a declared gap that prevents closure.
19
Chapter 19

Frequently asked questions

Short answers before you begin.

  • Do I need a paid plan? Essential practice can work without one; provider conditions change.
  • Does AI learn for me? No. It structures, proposes and supports review; understanding and decisions remain human.
  • Can I use any source? Only authorised, relevant and traceable sources.
  • When do I stop? When evidence is sufficient for the purpose and residual risk is declared.
